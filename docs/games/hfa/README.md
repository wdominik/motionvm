# Hilfe für Amajambere

*Hilfe für Amajambere — this page introduces the game and indexes its documentation. The engine it runs on is documented under [MOTION 16-bit](../../README.md#motion-16-bit).*

A point-and-click adventure about development aid, in which the player is an
agricultural expert sent to a highland region whose harvests are failing. It
is the one game of the five where the subject matter *is* the game: it is
carried almost entirely by dialogue rather than by puzzles.

|---|---|

| Made by | ART DEPARTMENT WA GmbH, Bochum |

| Display | 320×200 in 256 colors |
| Given away | Freeware, copyable unmodified for at most 5 DM |

## What it is about

**Amajambere** is a region of the fictional country **Ubuntaka**, and its
villages — Barabarabengi, Mihanatoke, Kijijimbegu, Gombeshamba, Majikitoyo —
are losing their soil: heavy rain washes the fertile earth off the slopes and
the sun finishes what is left. The player is **Paul Lerche**, whom everyone
addresses as *Mudagi*, and the work is to get multi-level cultivation accepted
by the people who would have to do it.

That framing is what makes the game unusual here. Its opposition is not a
villain but disagreement: who decides what a village needs, whether a bridge
should be built the expensive way or as an *Irish Crossing*, and what happens
when an expert ignores the chief. One of the game's own letters puts it
plainly — *"Ich kann nicht begreifen, wie John es fertiggebracht hat, Chief
Kalalas Ansichten in einer solch heiklen Angelegenheit zu ignorieren."*
A second thread runs on the women's earnings from cattle and goats and the
protest that proposing they keep them provokes.

Twenty locations carry it. **The game opens on location 20**, its front page,
with the menu up, and location 11 is the travel map that moves between the
rest ([game structure](game-structure.md)). The named cast includes Chief
Kalala, Josefu, Antony, John and Bomba; the prompts address the player as
*Sie* while the dialogue uses *Du*.

## Who made it

The game ships no credits table. The attribution is in its license file,
`FREEWARE.TXT`, which places the copyright with **ART DEPARTMENT WA GmbH of
Bochum**, says the game was made for the BMZ, and permits unmodified copying
for at most 5 DM but not inclusion in CD-ROM or disc-magazine compilations
without consent. The integrity checker `VRCHKSUM.EXE` carries the same house
in a binary string, *"(C) … The Art Department"*
([other shipped files](other-files.md)).

The same agency makes [Dunkle Schatten 2](../ds2/README.md) the following year
and [Die Enviro-Kids greifen ein](../enviro/README.md) alongside it.

## Why it exists

An edutainment title for a federal ministry, addressed at young players and
given away as freeware. The client is in the game's name twice over — the
ministry commissioned it, and its acronym is the name of the player binary.
`LIESMICH.DOK` is a printable reply card to the ministry in Bonn, carrying a
competition of six trips there and sixty information packages, closing
1995-12-31.
