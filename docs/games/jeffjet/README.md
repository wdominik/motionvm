# Jeff Jet - Abenteuer InfoHighway

*Jeff Jet - Abenteuer InfoHighway — this page introduces the game and indexes its documentation. The engine it runs on is documented under [MOTION 16-bit](../../README.md#motion-16-bit).*

A point-and-click adventure that takes place **inside a computer**. Two people
are scanned into the machine by accident and have to find the way out through
its parts — the memory, the processor, the printer's composing room — while
the hardware, which has opinions, gets in the way.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Released | 1995 by its launcher's own sign-off; the shipped files are re-stamped 1998-04-10 |
| Commissioned by | Hewlett Packard GmbH |
| Made by | Promotion Software GmbH, Tübingen |
| Engine | MOTION 16-bit — `HPPLAY.EXE`, the second of the four builds |
| Display | 320×200 in 256 colors |
| Given away | Free of charge |

## What it is about

**Jeff** and **Donna** go into the machine through a scanner and come out of
a DeskJet: the printer is the way home, and getting to it is the game. Thirteen
locations carry it, twelve of them inside the hardware and the thirteenth the
room the game starts in ([module map](module-map.md)).

The cast is the machine's own components given rank and personality. Chief
among them is a general whose name is the joke the game is built on —
**General Claus Peter Unit** — and the closing text disposes of the whole cast
in one line each: the Antarctic researcher who got so used to the cold that
afterwards *"er sich danach nur noch in Tiefkühltruhen herumtrieb"*; the
archaeologist whose four-hundred-page book about a potsherd was *"ein totaler
Mißerfolg"*; Major Tom, demoted to caretaker of the underwater station; the
General and his wife, now *"Eltern einer neuen Generation von Prozessoren"*;
Donna, at work on a program that puts the user directly into a computer game;
and Jeff, preparing to emigrate to New Guinea in case she succeeds.

The advertisement is in the furniture rather than the dialogue: *Hewlett
Packard* is said nine times, *Drucker* twenty-two and *Modem* eighteen
([game structure](game-structure.md)).

## Who made it

The game credits itself in text table 14, as the last thing it does — the
narrator remembers them at the end: *"Was red' ich da? Es fehlen ja noch die
Credits!"*

| Role | Named |
|---|---|
| Konzipiert und realisiert | Promotion Software GmbH, im Auftrag der Hewlett Packard GmbH |
| Programmierung | Stefan Hoffmann, Frank Fischell |
| Art Direction | Thomas Andrae |
| Hintergrundgrafiken | Thomas Andrae, Erik Kutschke |
| Animation | Animationsstudio Ludewig, Thomas Andrae, Michael Hoffmann, Christian Krämer |
| Grafik gescannt und bearbeitet | Michael "Scan-Wizard" Hoffmann; Frank Fischell prepared the 1517 single graphics |
| Musik | Jochen Heß, Norbert Schmidt |
| Dialoge | Michael Stigler wrote the bulk of the 3000 lines; Stefan Hoffmann and Frank Fischell the rest |
| Support | Michael Stigler; and Heiko Kaspers' mailbox, for distributing the demos |
| Testing | Stefan Mayer, Frank Berg, Michael Stigler, Niko Vasilakis, Michael Toennies, Thomas Andrae, *Chief* and *der Seatroll* |

The launcher says the same thing more briefly: `HP.BAT` echoes *"(c) 1995
Hewlett Packard"* and *"Entwickelt von Promotion Software GmbH, TM-übingen"* —
the typo is the shipped one ([other shipped files](other-files.md)).

**Michael Stigler** is worth noting across games: a *Michel "Babe" Stigler*
closes [Victor Loomes'](../vloomes/README.md) credits beside the engine's
version, and this game — the same studio, two years later — credits a Michael
Stigler for its dialogue and support ([open questions](../../open-questions.md#motion-16-bit)).
Jochen Heß and Norbert Schmidt turn up in
[Dunkle Schatten 2](../ds2/README.md) too, as the authors of a library it
credits.

## Why it exists

An advergame for Hewlett Packard, made when the *Informationshighway* was the
word of the year, given away rather than sold. Its purpose is served by
premise rather than by lecture: the player spends the whole game inside the
client's product line and leaves through the client's printer.
